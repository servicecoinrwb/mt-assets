🎬 The Install Presentation — Runbook

The visit, in order: what to say first, what to show when, and how to close. Residential replacement, packet in hand.

The whole visit on one line

  1. Arrive & connect — their problem, their words
  2. Look at the equipment — earn the diagnosis
  3. Present the fix — scope before price
  4. The price, both ways — total AND monthly, then silence
  5. The protection story — fourteen words, packet open
  6. Close — two small questions, never one big one
  7. Lock it — deposit/signature/schedule before you leave
1

Arrive & Connect

~3 min
Goal: they talk, you listen. You're a guest, not a salesman.
Thanks for having me out — before I look at anything, tell me what's been going on with it. When did you first notice?
  • Let them tell the whole story — the noise, the bill, the room that never cools. Write down their exact words (you'll use them in stage 3).
  • Ask the two planning questions early, casually: "How long are you planning to be in the house?" and "Anybody in the house sensitive to heat/cold — kids, older folks?" — both feed the close later.
Don't mention price, brands, or financing yet. Don't diagnose from the kitchen. The person who listens first wins the trust the price needs later.
2

Look at the Equipment

~10 min
Goal: earn the right to recommend. Twenty years of eyes on their specific system.
  • Inspect furnace + condenser with them if they'll follow you — narrate what you see in plain words: "see this rust line here — that's the heat exchanger sweating," "this compressor's pulling more amps than it was built for."
  • Photograph the data plates (feeds DSM later) and anything visibly failing — the photo becomes your exhibit in stage 3.
  • If they don't follow, come back with one physical thing to show: the phone photo, the filter, the reading on your meter.
The age line: "This unit's from [year] — it's given you [X] years, which is honestly a full career for this equipment. Nothing you did wrong; it's just done." Removes blame, frames replacement as natural, not a failure they caused.
3

Present the Fix — Scope Before Price

~5 min
Goal: they understand exactly what they're buying BEFORE hearing what it costs.
📄 PROPOSAL (scope section)
Here's what I'd do, start to finish. [Walk the scope: remove old, new Goodman system, new lineset, permits, commissioning, haul-away.] And remember what you told me about [their exact words from stage 1] — this is what fixes that specifically.
  • Use their words from stage 1 — "the upstairs bedroom," "the July bill" — tying every scope item to a complaint they voiced.
  • Mention the Goodman insert exists but don't read it — "the equipment details are all in here for you."
  • Exclusions out loud, briefly — "this assumes your ductwork's good, which it is." Honest scope reads as competence.
Don't let them flip to the price page yet — hand the proposal AFTER you've walked the scope, or hold it and walk them through it in your order, not theirs.
4

The Price — Both Ways, Then Silence

~2 min
Goal: one clean number, one monthly number, zero flinching, then SHUT UP.
All of that — equipment, install, permits, haul-away — is [total], all-in, no surprises. Or if monthly works better for your budget, that's around [total ÷ 50] a month. (then silence — count to five in your head)
  • Say the number flat and unapologetic, like you've said it four hundred times. Any hedging ("I know it's a lot...") teaches them to negotiate.
  • The silence is the technique. Whoever talks first after the price reveals their position. Let them react.
  • If they flinch at the total but not the monthly → financing conversation (stage 4b below). If they nod → straight to stage 5.
4b — if the total stalls them: "The monthly check takes about two minutes and doesn't touch your credit — I can text you the link right now and you'll see your real options while I'm standing here." 📄 FINANCING ONE-PAGER — hand it, text the Acorn link (T💳 in lead-texts), keep going.
5

The Protection Story

~4 min
Goal: fourteen words, then let the paper sell the plan. This is where you're different from every other bid.
And here's the part nobody else quoting you is going to say: ten years parts, two years labor included — five on our plan, and I can lock the full ten if you want it.
📄 WARRANTY TIER SHEET 📄 BROCHURE
  • Open the tier sheet, point at the ladder — "included, included, with-the-plan, one-time option" — thirty seconds, no lecture.
  • The registration line: "and I register the equipment FOR you — most contractors leave that as your homework, and unregistered cuts your parts coverage in half."
  • The plan, sold as maintenance: "$29 a month or $329 for the year — that's the two tune-ups, and it's what keeps every warranty in this stack valid."
  • The 10-year, if financing's in play: "about $28 more a month locks labor for the full decade." Attach rates live in that sentence.
Don't explain all four tiers exhaustively — point at the sheet and let them read. The objection answers are printed in the brochure FAQ; if they ask, point at the answer.
6

The Close — Two Small Questions

~2 min
Goal: never ask "so do you want it?" Ask two small choices that assume the yes.
So — two quick decisions and I can get you on the schedule: do you want the protection plan riding along, and do you want the ten-year locked in? Everything else is my problem.
  • Both questions are choices within the sale, not the sale itself. Answering either one IS the yes.
  • If they're deciding between bids: "totally fair — one thing to compare apples-to-apples: ask the other guys what their labor warranty is and who registers the equipment. I'll wait." (nobody else answers that well)
  • If it's a spouse-decision: leave the packet, offer to come back or hop on a call tonight, and send the T💳 or a recap text before you leave the driveway — the follow-up starts while the driveway's still warm.

✅ YES

Deposit or signature on the tier sheet (Warranty Tier line filled in!), schedule the install date before leaving, plan signup link texted on the spot if they took it. Confirm date by text within the hour.

🕐 NOT YET

Packet stays with them, recap text sent from the truck, follow-up scheduled in DSM (playbook: day 2 nudge, day 5 check-in). A live quote with a monthly number attached rarely dies quietly.

7

Lock It & Leave Clean

~3 min
Goal: nothing left ambiguous. They know what happens next and when.
Here's what happens from here: [date] we're on site by [time], the whole job's about [duration], I register everything with the manufacturer that week, and your paperwork — warranty certificate, registration confirmation — comes with the final invoice. You'll never wonder where anything stands.
  • In DSM before you drive off: job scheduled, warranty tier set on the job record, plan link sent if sold, proposal status flipped.
  • Same-day text: "Great meeting you — you're on the books for [date]. Anything comes up before then, this number reaches me directly."
The review seed, planted at the close: "When we're done, if we've earned it, a Google review helps a small shop like mine more than any ad — I'll only ask once." Planting it now makes the post-install ask feel expected, not extractive.

Papers in the packet, in firing order

  1. Proposal — stage 3 (scope walk) and stage 4 (the number)
  2. Financing one-pager — stage 4b, only if the total stalls
  3. Warranty tier sheet — stage 5, the ladder + the signature line
  4. Brochure — stage 5, leave-behind that answers objections overnight
  5. Goodman insert — referenced stage 3, lives in the packet as depth

Everything stays in the packet whether they buy or not — the packet is the salesman that stays after you leave.
