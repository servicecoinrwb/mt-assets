💳 Financing — Practice Sheet

The one sentence that saves stalled quotes. Read twice, say the scripts out loud, drill without peeking.

The whole thing in two sentences

1. You are not a lender — Acorn is. Their pre-qual, their lenders, their paperwork, their risk. You get paid in full when the job's done.

2. Your entire job is one sentence and a link: turn a scary lump sum into a familiar monthly number, and let the customer check their options in two minutes without touching their credit.

The sentence (memorize this)

That's [total] all-in — or if monthly works better for you, most folks finance a system like this for around [monthly estimate] a month. Takes about two minutes to see your actual options, and checking doesn't affect your credit. Want me to send you the link?

Then stop talking. You've turned "can I afford $9,400" into "$190 a month" — a question they already know how to answer.

Quick math — the driveway estimate

Rough monthly ≈ total ÷ 50 for a 5-year term at typical rates. It's an estimate — say "around" and let the pre-qual show real numbers.

÷50 approximates 60 months at ~9-10% APR. Actual offers vary by credit and lender — always say "around" and "your actual options will show in the pre-qual."

Never quote a rate or promise approval. You don't know their credit and you're not the lender. "The pre-qual shows your actual offers" is always the honest answer — and it's a better answer, because it's their real number, not your guess.

The objections (word-for-word answers)

"Will checking hurt my credit?"
No — the pre-qualification is a soft check, it doesn't touch your score. You only get a hard pull if you actually pick an offer and move forward, same as any loan.
"What's the interest rate?"
Depends on your credit — that's exactly what the two-minute check shows you: your real offers from multiple lenders, side by side. I've seen everything from promo rates on up. Better than me guessing at your business.
"I don't like debt / I'll just wait and save up."
Totally respect that — plenty of my customers pay cash. Just worth knowing: if the system's failing in July, waiting has its own cost — emergency pricing, no-cool days, sometimes a second repair on the way to the replacement. The monthly option just means you don't have to choose between fixing it right and fixing it now. Your call either way.
"Is this some contractor financing scam with hidden fees?"
Fair to ask. It's Acorn Finance — a lending marketplace, not me. Real banks and credit unions compete for your loan, you see the actual terms before you agree to anything, and I don't see your financial info at all. I just get paid for the work when it's done, same as cash.

When to offer it (and when not)

Offer on: any residential replacement quote over ~$3K, any repair-vs-replace conversation ("$1,800 repair or $9,400 new — and new can be ~$190/mo"), and the moment you hear hesitation words: "let me think about it," "I need to talk to my spouse," "that's a lot right now."

Skip on: commercial/FM work (they PO it), small tickets under ~$1,500 (monthly on a capacitor sounds silly), and anyone who's already said they're paying cash — don't unsell a sale.

The killer combo: financing turns the warranty pitch into pocket change. "$9,400 — or about $190 a month. And for about $28 more a month, labor's covered for the full ten years." The 10-year JB at $1,395 stalls as a lump; at +$28/mo it attaches.

Drills — answer out loud, then check

D1. Customer at a $9,400 quote goes quiet and says "we need to think about it." Your next sentence?
Check answer
The sentence: "Totally understand — one thing worth knowing while you think: most folks finance a system like this for around $190 a month, and checking your options takes two minutes without touching your credit. Want me to text you the link so you have it?" You've left them with a smaller question and a live link instead of a dead quote.
D2. "What's the rate?" — answer without guessing a number.
Check answer
"Depends on your credit — the two-minute check shows your real offers from multiple lenders." Never quote a rate. Their pre-qual is the only honest number in the conversation.
D3. Quick math: $12,000 full system + $1,395 JB 10-year. Rough monthly for the package?
Check answer
$13,395 ÷ 50 ≈ ~$268/mo. Say "around $270 a month, everything included — equipment, install, and a decade of labor coverage."
D4. Customer asks "does applying hurt my credit?" — precise answer?
Check answer
Pre-qual = soft check, no score impact. Hard pull only happens if they choose an offer and proceed. Know this cold — it's the #1 hesitation and the answer is genuinely reassuring.
D5. Where does financing NOT belong?
Check answer
Commercial/FM quotes, tickets under ~$1,500, and customers who've already committed to cash. Offering it there reads as pushy or silly; skipping it there is judgment.
D6. Say the sentence from memory, right now, out loud — using $8,000 as the total.
Check answer
"That's $8,000 all-in — or if monthly works better, most folks finance a system like this for around $160 a month. Two minutes to see your options, doesn't touch your credit. Want the link?"
