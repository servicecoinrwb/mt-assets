🛡️ Warranty Sales — Practice Sheet

Read it twice. Say the scripts out loud. Then do the drills at the bottom without peeking.

The whole thing in three sentences

1. Every install already comes with 10-year parts (manufacturer, we register it) and 2-year labor (us, free).

2. The $29/mo maintenance plan is a maintenance product — two tune-ups a year. The 5-year labor extension is a perk of being on it, alive only while the plan is.

3. The 10-year labor policy is a separate one-time purchase (~$1,395 on the quote), insured by JB. Bought once, locked forever, transfers with the house.

The one idea that makes it click: RENT vs BUY

🔵 5-YEAR = RENTED

It exists because their plan is active. Stop paying $29/mo → the extension evaporates, coverage falls back to the 2-year window. It's a membership perk, like lounge access with a credit card. They never "buy 5 years" — they hold it.

🟡 10-YEAR = BOUGHT

One line item, one payment, done. JB insures it. Nothing to keep current (except maintenance, which every warranty requires anyway). It's theirs even if they sell the house. This is the only warranty they ever actually purchase.

If you remember nothing else: the plan is not a warranty product. It's a maintenance product that carries a warranty perk. Say "the plan comes with" — never "the plan costs $29 for 5 years of warranty."

The driveway pitch (memorize this one)

Your install comes with the manufacturer's 10-year parts warranty — I register that for you — plus 2 years of labor from me, free. Most of my customers go on the maintenance plan: $29 a month, two tune-ups a year, priority scheduling, and while you're on it your labor coverage runs 5 years instead of 2. And if you want labor locked in for the full 10 no matter what, that's a one-time option — I'll put the number on your quote.

Then stop talking. Two yes/no questions are now on the table: plan? and lock the decade? Let them pick.

The killer objection (this WILL come up)

"The first 2 years are free — why am I paying for the plan during years I'm already covered?"
Flip it: the plan was never buying warranty.
Fair question — but the $29 isn't buying warranty years, it's buying the two tune-ups. Bought one at a time those visits run $250-300 a year, so the plan is the maintenance at a discount. And here's the thing most people don't know: your manufacturer's 10-year parts warranty requires documented maintenance — it's in every brand's fine print. Skip the tune-ups, and a year-6 coil claim can get denied. So the plan protects the ten years of coverage you already own, starting day one. The 5-year labor extension is just my thank-you on top.
"I'll just start the plan in year 2 then."
The 30-day rule.
The 5-year extension only applies when the plan starts within 30 days of install — same reason you can't buy flood insurance while the basement's filling. The extension is priced for a system that's been maintained its whole life. You can join the plan anytime for the maintenance itself — but the extended labor coverage is a day-one decision.
"Why would I buy the 10-year if the plan gives me 5?"
Rent vs buy, out loud.
The 5 rides on the plan — cancel the plan, it goes with it, and it ends at year five either way. The 10-year is bought once and it's yours no matter what — insured by a national warranty company, and it even transfers free if you sell the house. Years 6 through 10 are when compressors and coils actually fail, and that's exactly what it covers. Different animals: one's a perk, one's a policy.

Drills — answer out loud, then check

D1. Customer takes the plan at install, cancels it in month 30, blower motor dies in month 40. Covered?
Check answer
No. Plan lapsed → extension died with it → coverage reverted to the 2-year window, which ended at month 24. (Parts may still be covered by the manufacturer IF maintenance was documented — labor is on them.) This is why the lapse alert pings your phone.
D2. Customer bought the 10-year, never joined the plan, compressor dies year 7. What happens?
Check answer
Probably covered, with a risk. JB pays the labor claim — BUT JB and the manufacturer both require maintenance records. If the system was visibly neglected, claims can be contested. This is why even 10-year buyers should take the plan: the policy covers the money, the plan protects the validity.
D3. What does the customer actually receive for $29/mo? (List it without saying "warranty" first.)
Check answer
Two seasonal tune-ups (~$250-300 retail value), priority scheduling, no trip charge on covered calls, repair discount — and then the 5-year labor extension as the perk. Maintenance first, warranty last. If you lead with the warranty, you invite the "why pay during free years" objection.
D4. $9K install, they take plan + 10-year. What's the quote total, what recurs, and what's your JB cost?
Check answer
Quote = $10,395 ($9,000 + $1,395 JBALTH line). Recurring = $29/mo Stripe, separate. Your JB cost ≈ $1,040 → ~$355 margin on the warranty line, plus $348/yr plan revenue, plus a customer locked in for a decade.
D5. Year-4 no-cool call comes in. First three things you check in DSM?
Check answer
① Search the job → read the Warranty line (tier / policy # / registered date). ② If tier = 5yr-plan → Contracts page: plan active? ③ If 10yr → note the JB policy #, check pre-auth rules before big repairs. Then: covered = no labor charge, no trip charge, smile.
D6. Say the entire driveway pitch from memory. Right now. Out loud.
Check answer
10-yr parts registered by us → 2-yr labor free → plan: $29/mo, two tune-ups, coverage runs 5 while on it → full-10 lock available, number on the quote. Then stop talking.
